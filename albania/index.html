WHAT IS ELISENCE?

A person-centred health, care and human-continuity ecosystem designed to connect everyday life, family, health intelligence, professional care and institutional systems around one continuous journey.

ELISENCE is not a conventional healthcare application.

It is not a collection of isolated health features placed behind a single interface.

And it was not designed around one disease, one provider, one device, one institution or one moment of care.

ELISENCE was designed around a different unit of continuity:

the person.

A person does not experience health as a sequence of disconnected systems.

They live one life.

They move between home, work, family, pharmacy, primary care, hospitals, laboratories, medication, wellbeing, chronic-condition management, specialist care, travel, recovery, prevention, research and public-health environments.

The systems around them, however, are often fragmented.

Information is distributed.

Responsibilities are separated.

Context is lost.

The same story may need to be repeated.

A medication may exist in one system.

A symptom in another.

A laboratory result somewhere else.

A hospital sees one episode.

A family member sees another.

A pharmacist sees the prescription.

A doctor sees the consultation.

A wearable sees signals.

A researcher sees data.

A ministry sees population-level indicators.

Yet the individual remains the only person expected to carry the full continuity of the journey.

ELISENCE was built to address that gap.

Its purpose is to create a trusted digital environment in which health, care, context and participation can remain connected across time — without collapsing the distinct responsibilities, authorities and ownership boundaries of the systems involved.

CONNECTED — NOT COLLAPSED

A critical architectural principle of ELISENCE is that connection does not mean ownership.

Systems can communicate.

Systems can provide context to one another.

Systems can consume outputs from other domains.

But one subsystem does not automatically inherit the authority of another.

This distinction is fundamental.

Sensor Infrastructure ≠ Medical Intelligence

Sensor and signal infrastructure may provide physiological or contextual input.

Medical Intelligence may consume relevant information.

But Medical Intelligence does not become the owner of the sensor infrastructure.

Medical Tracking ≠ Medical Intelligence

Tracking can record states, progress and history.

It can consume intelligence outputs.

But it does not become the authority that owns the intelligence itself.

Medical Intelligence ≠ Prescription Intelligence

Medication reference, medication intelligence, prescription intelligence, prescribing workflow and prescribing authority remain distinct concepts.

A system capable of providing medication information does not thereby gain authority to prescribe.

GP / Primary Care ≠ Medical Intelligence

Primary care can consume relevant context and contribute to the wider journey without becoming the owner of the Medical Intelligence architecture.

ELISA ≠ Every Domain Engine

ELISA can act as the longitudinal companion and interaction layer around the individual.

That does not mean ELISA becomes the owner of every specialist intelligence domain.

MODULARITY IS A GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE, NOT ONLY AN ENGINEERING DECISION

ELISENCE is intentionally designed as a modular multi-system ecosystem.

That matters technically.

But it matters even more institutionally.

A country may accept one capability and reject another.

A regulator may require one function to remain disabled.

A health system may wish to pilot only selected modules.

A ministry may want population analytics without individual-level visibility.

A hospital may use clinical communication while not adopting another ELISENCE subsystem.

A jurisdiction may prohibit a workflow that another jurisdiction allows.

A professional capability may be permitted only for appropriately verified roles.

ELISENCE is therefore designed around the idea that:

capability, access, authority, purpose and jurisdiction should not be treated as the same thing.

A capability can exist technically without being activated.

An engine can exist without being exposed to every actor.

A workflow can remain gated.

A subsystem can be turned off.

Another subsystem can continue operating.

This is important because national healthcare infrastructure should not require an all-or-nothing adoption model.

AFFORDABILITY WAS PART OF THE ARCHITECTURAL QUESTION

Can useful longitudinal health intelligence be made accessible without requiring every person to purchase premium wearable hardware?

TRUST & GOVERNANCE

The Foundation Beneath Every Track

Health-data collaboration cannot begin with technology and add governance later.

Governance must be part of the architecture from the beginning.

Albania’s Law No. 124/2024 on Personal Data Protection establishes the country’s current personal-data framework and expressly aligns key elements with the EU GDPR framework. Albania’s Information and Data Protection Commissioner supervises implementation and international data-transfer requirements.

Any future ELISENCE collaboration involving personal or health information would therefore need to be designed around applicable Albanian law, relevant European requirements and the specific legal responsibilities of participating institutions.

We propose treating the following as a dedicated collaboration workstream rather than a compliance appendix:

PRIVACY

  • Purpose limitation.
  • Data minimisation.
  • Appropriate lawful basis.
  • Transparency.
  • Individual rights.
  • Retention controls.

CONSENT & PARTICIPATION

  • Clear participation models.
  • Understandable information.
  • Ability to withdraw where applicable.
  • No hidden expansion of use.

DATA PROVENANCE

  • Where did information originate?
  • Who created it?
  • When was it created?
  • Has it changed?
  • Can its history be understood?

ACCESS CONTROL

  • Who should see what?
  • Under which role?
  • For which purpose?
  • For how long?

RESEARCH GOVERNANCE

  • Ethics.
  • Protocol design.
  • Institutional approval.
  • Pseudonymisation or de-identification where appropriate.
  • Controlled research access.

RESPONSIBLE AI

  • Human oversight.
  • Explainability appropriate to use.
  • Bias evaluation.
  • Performance monitoring.
  • Clear limitations.
  • No unsupported clinical authority.

CYBERSECURITY

  • Identity.
  • Authentication.
  • Authorisation.
  • Auditability.
  • Incident response.
  • Secure data handling.

CROSS-BORDER DATA GOVERNANCE

  • Any movement or access to personal information across jurisdictions must be assessed under the applicable legal framework rather than assumed to be permissible merely because organisations are collaborating internationally.

The purpose of this governance layer is not to slow innovation.

It is what makes responsible innovation possible.
